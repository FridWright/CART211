The Convenience vs. Privacy Trade-Off

Computational systems are inherently designed to collect data, forcing artists to weigh their privacy against their ability to reach an audience.

Instrumentation

John Maeda (2019) explains that to "speak machine" is to recognize that machine systems are fundamentally instrumented (equipped with sensors, measurement tools, or monitoring devices). These instruments allow the system to collect data about itself or its environment in real time.

The system's ability to operate, improve, and provide tailored services is entirely dependent on its capacity to capture and record data about its environment and its users.

Omotenashi

This instrumentation is what allows the system to "know you better" to "serve you better," achieving a form of data-driven hospitality called Omotenashi. Omotenashi is about anticipating a customer's needs without having to ask.

In the machine world, this is achieved through data.

The Paradox

Maeda (2019) states that "the more privacy you give up, the more convenience you get." When users share information about themselves, they are "guaranteed the pleasure of getting what you want instead of feeling the pain of being served incorrectly."

The exchange between personal information and computational convenience is delicate. For artists, this means the utility and ability to provide tailored services, like connecting with a niche audience, is entirely dependent on this data extraction.
