The Monopolized Canvas

How a few dominant technology companies have replaced traditional cultural spaces and now dictate the conversation around art.

De Facto Monopolies

Tech platforms like Facebook, Google, Spotify, Netflix, etc. function as de facto monopolies that control where art is seen and discussed.

They have effectively replaced the traditional public square, which is dangerous because they operate primarily under a profit motive.

"Shadow Banning"

The consequence? If art is deemed "uncomfortable or inconvenient," these platforms are able to simply remove it from their platforms.

The instiutions that have large influence over these platforms and can tailor what is "acceptable" to align with any political agenda. This concept is not new for artists, as can be exemplarily observed in the C.I.A.'s covert involvement in weaponizing and apoliticizing art in the Cold War Era. But the scope of today's censorship is at an entirely larger level of affect, due to the sheer amount of user's who engage with these platforms daily.'

Art as Defence

Artists must compromise their vision to reach an audience. Tech companies benefit from the fatalistic attitude that the digital future is inevitable, as it discourages critical thinking and resistance...art is the essential, fast-moving medium for challenging these systems.
