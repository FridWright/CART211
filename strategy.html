The Strategies of Expansion

The operational strategies surveillance capitalists use to secure their dominance and continuously expand their data-extraction architecture.

Phase One: The Strategic Goals

Kidnap

The act of unilaterally claiming human experience as free, proprietary raw material, bypassing individual rights, awareness, or comprehension.

Corner

The monopolistic effort to seize and control the crucial assets (the vast reservoirs of behavioural data) that fuel the predictive models. This is about securing the scale necessary for high-certainty prediction products.

Compete

The continuous struggle against rivals (both other surveillance capitalists and older forms of capitalism) and, critically, against any form of democratic or legislative resistance.

Phase Two: The Implementation Process

Incursion

The company launches a new service that, while offering value, simultaneously generates user data in a new and highly intimate way. This works as a form of dispossession, weakening user control over a specific part of their lives or environment.

Habituation

Users grow accustomed to these new tools and the underlying data extraction mechanisms. The initial surprise or discomfort fades into acceptance as the convenience of the service takes hold.

Adaptation

If public outcry or regulatory pressure builds, companies employ small, tactical changes to their user interfaces or privacy policies. These changes are designed to appear responsive while leaving the core data extraction and economic model fundamentally intact.

Expansion

The company continues to build upon the service, drawing attention away from data issues by focusing on innovation, utility, and growth. The overall goal is the continuous expansion of the extraction architecture into new domains (ex/wearable tech, smart homes, cars).
